Explore SkyClear end to end

A drone pilot asks "can I fly here?", gets an answer with the rule behind it, requests permission from the right officer, and gets alerted if something changes. Follow these steps to see all of it. It takes about ten minutes.

Before you start

  1. The app must be running. In a terminal in the project folder run ./run.sh --demo. It starts everything and prints your logins.
  2. Logins are generated on your machine, none are built in. To see them again run python backend/manage.py logins.
  3. One-time codes (sign-up, password reset) arrive in the mail catcher at localhost:8025. Open it in another tab.

1. As a visitor, no account needed

  1. Open Pilot. Press one of the example buttons, or click the map or search a place.
  2. Read the answer: the colour, what it means, the zone you are inside, and the exact rules behind it.
  3. Open the Route tab, press the example route, and run the simulation. A simulated drone flies the route and shows where the trouble is.
  4. Open the Ask tab and ask "Do I need permission to fly near an airport?". The answer is taken only from the rules, with the rule shown. Ask something unrelated and it says it could not find it.
  5. Try the Auto, Light and Dark switch at the top, and the Satellite button on the map.

2. As a new pilot

  1. Press Create account. Fill the form. A code is emailed to you, find it in the mail catcher and enter it.
  2. Open the Flights tab. The checklist shows what is left: verify your pilot certificate, add a drone, verify the drone.
  3. Upload a photo for each (any image works in development). They now wait for an officer.

3. As an officer

  1. Sign in with an officer login from manage.py logins. Open Authority.
  2. Open the Verification tab. View the documents and press Verify for the pilot and the drone.
  3. Each officer sees only requests for their own office. The administrator login sees every office.

4. Request permission, take off and fly

  1. Back as the pilot (now verified), check a spot inside a yellow zone, such as the government area example. The page shows which office will review it. Press Request permission.
  2. As the officer for that office, open Requests, set an altitude limit and conditions, and press Approve and issue permit.
  3. As the pilot, open Flights. Each flight shows its stages: Requested, Approved, Cleared, Flying, Landed, with the one next step written under it. The permit appears with a QR code.
  4. Press Run preflight. It passes only if every check passes now, and the flight moves to Cleared. If a check fails, the reasons are listed.
  5. Press Take off. A bar appears at the top with your drone's height, speed and battery, and your drone moves on the map.
  6. As an officer, open Live. The flight is in the air list, with its numbers and a Recall button.

5. A restriction appears, and the drone is called home

  1. As the administrator, open Publish, click the map over the drone, set a time window and publish a notice, for example a VIP movement. You can also press Run the VIP movement scenario in the Live tab, which does this over the drones now in the air.
  2. The pilot gets a red alert at once, then "Your drone entered restricted airspace". The notice lists any approved permits it conflicts with.
  3. Open Requests, All flights and revoke the permit (or press Recall in Live). The drone is ordered home, the pilot is told to land, and the flight ends as Recalled.
  4. Press Land now as the pilot. The flight log shows the times, the highest point and how many times restricted airspace was entered.

6. Fly it from your phone, on a big screen

  1. Run python backend/manage.py prepare-demo. It prepares a demo pilot with two approved flights.
  2. Sign in as that pilot and open Demo screen. Pick a flight and press Take off with phone control. A full-screen satellite view follows the drone.
  3. Scan the QR code with a phone on the same network. The phone becomes the controller: two sticks, Return home and Land. No sign-in needed.
  4. Climb past the permit's height limit or fly out of the dashed permit area: the banner turns red and the log records the breach. Full setup and a three minute script: docs/PHONE_DEMO.md.

7. Administrator only

  1. Open the Team tab to invite an official. You get a temporary password to hand over. They must choose their own at first sign-in.

What is simulated or sample

Rule text, the Secretariat and park boundaries, the office names and the VIP notice are sample data and say so. Airport zones are derived from open data. Drones in the simulation are not real. Registration here is not government registration. Live drone tracking and the analytics dashboard are being built by the team.
